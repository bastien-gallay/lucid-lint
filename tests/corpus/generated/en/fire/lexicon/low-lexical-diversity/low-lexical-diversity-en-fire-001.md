The garden needs water every morning, so every morning I walk into the garden and water the plants before the sun climbs high. The plants grow best when the soil stays wet, and the soil stays wet only if I water the garden early, because the sun dries the soil by noon. Green leaves mean the plants are growing, and growing plants need more water, so the greener the garden gets, the more water I carry. I planted the seeds in spring, watered the seeds, and watched the seeds grow into green plants with wide leaves. Some plants grow faster than other plants, but every plant in the garden needs the same three things: water, sun, and good soil. When the soil looks pale, I water it; when the leaves look pale, I water them too. My garden is a green garden because I water it every single morning, and the plants reward the watering by growing taller and greener. By summer the garden will be full of tall green plants, and I will still be out there every morning, watering the soil, watering the leaves, watching everything grow.
